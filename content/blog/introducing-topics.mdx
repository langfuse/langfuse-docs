## Let Topics find your unknown unknowns

Every team running an agent in production has the same question: "What do people do with my agent, and where does it fail?" The answer is in the traces, but there are more of them than anyone can read.

One way to find out is setting up evals when you know what to check for. For traces without evals, we see many teams let a coding agent find patterns. This works well on a smaller sample, but at production volume it is often too expensive. The result is then also local to the person running the analysis, does not update and does not integrate with other Langfuse features.

Topics continuously analyzes your traces, so you know sooner what to prioritize. It adds an enrichment layer on top of your traces, which gives your team and your agents a quick overview of what users do with your agents. Topics highlight common friction points unprompted, thus surfacing opportunities to support what your users already try to do.

## What you can do with Topics

### See every trace through facets

Each **facet** asks a different question about a trace. Choose the questions you want to explore.

- **Intent** is what the agent was asked to do.
- **Sentiment** is how the user felt about the interaction.
- **Outcome** is how the trace ended and whether the result is confirmed.
- **Issues** is the main problem in how the trace was handled.

Imagine two travelers. One wants an earlier departure. The other wants to fly on Sunday instead of Friday. Their Intent **facet summaries** describe the two requests, and both can land in a **topic** called **Flight changes**. A booking tool failure could affect both flight changes and hotel reservations. The Issues facet can bring those failures together in one topic, giving you a specific problem to test for.

### Explore topics in auto-refreshing maps or one-off snapshots

You can view the topics of a facet as a treemap or a scatter plot in the Langfuse UI.

Live maps can be used to continuously scan production traffic for new topics. Snapshots allow you to run a one-off topic analysis on a (filtered) set of traces. Both cluster the traces that match the specified filter and sample rate. You can have multiple live maps and snapshots at once. We recommend setting up one live map per agent or environment, so that each gets its own topics. Snapshots are useful for getting topics once for a filtered set, for example to pit your French-region versus your German-region customer support interactions.

### Go from a topic to a fix

A topic tells you where to look, so open it and read a few of its traces before you act. The fix can be the prompt, missing context for the agent, a gap in its knowledge base or a new evaluator. Filter the tracing table by the topic to work through its traces.

### Give your agents the enrichment layer

[Halo](/docs/api-and-data-platform/features/halo), the assistant inside Langfuse, uses Topics, and you can build your own agents and workflows on top of them through the [API](/docs/api-and-data-platform/features/public-api), [MCP](/docs/api-and-data-platform/features/mcp-server) and [CLI](/docs/api-and-data-platform/features/cli). Instead of reading raw traces to find patterns, an agent starts from available topics and loads full traces only where it needs them, to keep costs down. Ask your own agent: "Look at Langfuse Topics. What are the most common issues in my support agent, and which requests cause them?" Your agent can then open the traces behind a topic and find the step that caused the failure.

## What Topics showed us on Halo

We also run Topics on Halo's own traces, and it found things nobody on our team was looking for. The topics showed some people used Halo to write homework and Python scripts. A larger group tried to pen-test it, for example by asking for its environment variables.

Another topic was full of traces where Halo wrote its answer in its reasoning and the user saw nothing. We had no evaluator for either finding, and now we know which ones to set up.

## How Topics works

Once a trace goes idle, one model request can analyze all its selected facets. Topics turns each text summary into an **embedding** so it can group summaries with similar meanings within the same facet. A stronger model names and describes each cluster. Those named groups are the topics you explore on the map. Live maps refresh daily or when you click **Refresh map**.

## Open source, on your own models, at production scale

Topics is built like the rest of Langfuse:

- It is open source and runs on self-hosted Langfuse as well as on Langfuse Cloud.
- You choose the models from your own [LLM connections](/docs/administration/llm-connection), and the calls go to your provider with your key.
- It is designed for production scale and tested at millions of traces per day.

## How to get started

Topics is available today in public beta on [Langfuse Cloud](/cloud), in all regions. Self-hosted deployments need Langfuse v4.

Open **Topics** in your project and click **Set up topics**. Choose the facets and your models, select which traces to cover, and start analyzing. The first topics appear about five minutes after setup.

On Langfuse Cloud, a trace with four new model-generated facet results adds four [billable units](/docs/observability/features/topics#models-and-costs), however many topics those results lead to. Reusing results in another map adds zero units. Your provider bills the model calls on your own key.

Read the [Topics docs](/docs/observability/features/topics) for the full setup.

Because Topics is in beta, it will still change, and we want your feedback in the [GitHub Discussion](https://github.com/orgs/langfuse/discussions/20801) to guide how. Thank you to everyone who asked us to build this and gave us feedback while we designed it.

Agent traces only get bigger. A run that was twelve spans last year is six hundred today — a fan-out of tool calls, a queue of embeddings, four retries, and a twenty-minute wait on a human somewhere in the middle. The old Timeline answered that by scrolling: you paged through a trace hoping to recognise the part that mattered.

The new **Responsive Timeline** starts from the other end. It fits the whole trace into the space you have — on both axes — so the first thing you see is the shape of the run, not its first screenful. Then you zoom into the interesting part like you would on a map.

## Geography of your time

Zoomed all the way out, a trace reads as terrain. Rows shrink as far as a single line, and colour carries the observation type, so a fan-out of tool calls, a wall of embeddings and one long generation are all recognisable before you have read a single label.

- **Scroll to pan, pinch or ⌘-scroll to zoom** — both axes together, time and rows.
- **Double-click any observation** to fly to it at a readable size. Names, the tree gutter and the duration labels come back on the way in.
- **Drag to select a time range**, or hit **Fit** to go back to the whole trace.
- **Hover the left edge** to peek at names without moving a single bar.

## Pinpoint the interesting detail

Whatever a bar has room for, it shows: how long it took, what it cost, and — for streaming generations — a mark inside the bar at the first token, so time-to-first-token is something you see rather than something you compute. When a bar is too narrow to say any of that, hovering it does.

Long waits stay honest. Idle time between spans is drawn to scale, which is usually the real answer to "why did this run take four minutes".

## It fits your screen too

The same view runs in the trace panel, in the side peek, and on a phone — the layout adapts to the box it is given rather than assuming a desktop. A 320-pixel peek and a 4K pane are the same renderer at different densities.

<Callout type="info">
  The Responsive Timeline replaces the previous Timeline view. There is nothing
  to opt into: it is the Timeline tab for everyone on Langfuse Cloud, and ships
  to self-hosted deployments with the next release.
</Callout>

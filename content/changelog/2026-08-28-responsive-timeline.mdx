Agent traces only get bigger. A run that was twelve spans last year is six hundred today — a fan-out of tool calls, a queue of embeddings, four retries, and a twenty-minute wait on a human somewhere in the middle. The old Timeline answered that by scrolling: you paged through a trace hoping to recognise the part that mattered.

The new **Responsive Timeline** fits the whole trace into the space you have, on both axes. You see the shape of the run first, then zoom into the part that matters like a map.

## Geography of your time

Zoomed out, a trace reads as terrain: rows shrink to a single line and colour carries the observation type, so a fan-out, a wall of embeddings and one long generation are recognisable before you read a label.

- **Scroll to pan, pinch or ⌘-scroll to zoom** — both axes together, time and rows.
- **Double-click any observation** to fly to it at a readable size. Names, the tree gutter and the duration labels come back on the way in.
- **Drag to select a time range**, or hit **Fit** to go back to the whole trace.
- **Hover the left edge** to peek at names without moving a single bar.

## It fits your screen too

The same view runs in the trace panel, in the side peek, and on a phone — the layout adapts to the box it is given rather than assuming a desktop. A 320-pixel peek and a 4K pane are the same renderer at different densities.

## Feedback

Traces will keep growing, and this view is built to keep up with whatever 2026 ships next. Tell us where it stops keeping up — what you look for in a big trace, and what you still cannot find — in our [feedback discussion](https://github.com/orgs/langfuse/discussions).

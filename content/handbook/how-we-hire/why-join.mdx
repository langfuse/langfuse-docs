# Why join Langfuse

This is the long version of the pitch on our [careers page](/careers), plus the questions candidates ask us most. Anyone who talks to candidates can use it.

## Why people join [#why]

### We're winning the category through open source [#open-source]

Langfuse is the [most used open-source AI engineering platform](/blog/2024-11-most-used-oss-llmops), with the largest distribution and scale in our space. Our [adoption and community metrics](/careers#public-metrics) are public. Being open source is our biggest advantage, and it's how we plan to win the category.

### AI-native companies and large enterprises [#customers]

Some of the most AI-native companies, like [Ramp](/users/ramp), run on Langfuse. So do very large enterprises. In practice, you build for teams who push AI engineering forward and for organizations with strict requirements on scale, security, and self-hosting, often in the same feature. See the [companies building with Langfuse](/users).

### You work directly with customers [#customer-contact]

Every engineer talks to customers in shared Slack Connect channels. Customers also review our RFCs before we build, so their feedback goes straight to the person making the decision. You find out quickly whether what you shipped helps. See [Shipping & Talking to Customers Above Little Else](/handbook/how-we-work/principles).

### We build in public [#community]

A lot of what we build starts as a thread in [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/orgs/langfuse/discussions) or an issue. Engineers answer users there themselves, write the docs for their features, and post about their work. New features have to work for self-hosters on day one.

### You own an area and compound over time [#ownership]

Every engineer owns a feature area. You decide what to build and why, write the RFC, build it, write the docs, and talk about it in public. Every project has one owner, from the backlog until it ships.

An RFC usually lays out a few real options before we pick one. The Topics RFC, for example, compared processing traces from S3 with processing them inside ClickHouse. Decisions happen in writing, the best argument wins, and seniority doesn't settle anything. Open questions get resolved by the two or three people closest to the problem.

As the team grows, you get to stay with your area and go deeper. And because Langfuse is open source, your commits, docs, and changelog posts stay public. The longer you stay, the bigger that track record gets.

### ClickHouse backs us and stays out of the way [#clickhouse]

We [joined ClickHouse](/blog/joining-clickhouse) in January 2026 for two reasons. First, building a sales team for a developer tool is hard, and ClickHouse already has one that now sells Langfuse. Second, Langfuse runs on ClickHouse. Rather than build our own database team, we work with the people who build the database, in the same Slack.

We still set our own roadmap. ClickHouse's founders have taken companies public before, and they act like an involved board: they check our results and leave the day-to-day to us.

Next, we want to make Langfuse data available in ClickHouse Cloud and connect it with the observability data in ClickStack. We don't know yet how to do this well. It will matter a lot to our users, and it's one of the harder problems ahead of us.

### AI-pilled and remote [#culture]

Every engineer gets a very high AI budget for models and coding agents. We run our own coding agents and trace them in Langfuse. There are three scheduled meetings a week, and the rest happens in writing (see [Maker Schedule](/handbook/how-we-work/principles)). We work from San Francisco, from Berlin and remotely across Europe, and remotely in the US Eastern time zone (see [Working Remotely](/handbook/how-we-work/remote)).

## What you'll work on [#work]

### Challenging UI and UX for large, messy data [#ui-ux]

A single agent trace can have thousands of spans, each carrying megabytes of payload. Making that readable in a browser, and keeping it fast, is as much a design problem as an engineering one. Examples: the [Responsive Timeline](/changelog/2026-08-28-responsive-timeline), the [graph view](/changelog/2026-07-13-graph-view-modes), the [filter search bar](/changelog/2026-06-19-filter-search-bar), [Pulse](/changelog/2026-07-28-langfuse-pulse), and the [redesigned session view](/changelog/2026-10-13-redesigned-session-view).

### Data infrastructure at petabyte scale [#data-infrastructure]

Langfuse Cloud stores petabytes of tracing data for thousands of teams, and self-hosters run the same codebase. The infrastructure is fully automated in Terraform, and we are moving ingestion to Rust in stages. Read [How Langfuse runs ClickHouse at agent scale](/resources/engineering/clickhouse-at-agent-scale) and [Simplifying Langfuse for Scale](/blog/2026-03-10-simplify-langfuse-for-scale).

### AI agents inside the product [#ai-agents]

We build agents for people who build agents. [Halo](/docs/halo), our in-app agent, has access to all the data in a project and runs code over thousands of observations in a sandbox. The [roadmap](/docs/roadmap) includes proactive issue detection with Topics and a more capable in-app agent.

### Tooling for AI engineers, like evals at scale [#tooling]

We build the tools teams use to improve their AI applications: [code evaluators](/blog/2026-06-22-code-evaluators-execution-model) that run untrusted code safely, LLM-as-a-judge on production traffic, [experiments](/changelog/2026-10-19-experiments) with CI/CD gates, the [CLI](/docs/api-and-data-platform/features/cli), the [MCP server](/docs/api-and-data-platform/features/mcp-server), and the upcoming Langfuse Gateway.

## Teams [#teams]

Product engineers build features end to end. That covers the UI, the agents inside Langfuse, evaluation tooling, and the SDKs and integrations that get data into Langfuse.

The data platform team owns ingestion, ClickHouse query performance, data processing, and the infrastructure behind Langfuse Cloud and self-hosting, from Helm charts to Terraform.

## Questions candidates often ask [#faq]

**How big is the team?** We keep the team small on purpose. You can see everyone on the [careers page](/careers).

**How independent are you from ClickHouse?** We set our own roadmap and run our own processes. ClickHouse holds us accountable for results.

**How much frontend work is there?** Product engineers build features end to end, and much of the hardest work is in the UI. You don't need to be a frontend specialist, but you should enjoy building interfaces people use every day.

**Do I need ClickHouse experience?** No. Data platform engineers learn it on the job, with ClickHouse's engineers one message away.

**What's the interview process?** See [Hiring Process](/handbook/how-we-hire/hiring-process).

## What we look for [#what-we-look-for]

Engineers here make a lot of decisions without anyone reviewing them, so we hire for that.

We look for people who want to go deep on one area and keep getting better at it. You should be able to think through a complex feature on your own and explain your decisions clearly in writing. We usually look for people who have led complex work before, often as a tech lead or founding engineer.

See also our [hiring philosophy](/handbook/how-we-hire/philosophy).

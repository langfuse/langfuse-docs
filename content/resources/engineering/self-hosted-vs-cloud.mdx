# Langfuse self-hosted vs. Cloud: how to choose

Langfuse runs in two ways: as a managed service ([Langfuse Cloud](https://cloud.langfuse.com)) or [self-hosted](/self-hosting) on your own infrastructure under an MIT license. Both run the same codebase and the same deployment architecture, so this is not a feature-tier decision in disguise. It is a decision about who operates the infrastructure and where your data lives.

**TL;DR:** Choose Cloud unless something specific forces your hand: a hard data-residency or network-isolation requirement that none of the four Cloud regions satisfies, a compliance regime your legal team maps to "our VPC only", or an existing platform team that already operates ClickHouse-class infrastructure. Self-hosting is a real, unrestricted option (all core features, MIT license, no scale ceiling), but you take on genuine operations work. Teams that pick self-hosting for soft reasons often migrate to Cloud later; teams with hard requirements never regret starting self-hosted.

## The actual trade-off

In practice the decision comes down to one exchange: **legal and procurement effort versus infrastructure effort.**

- With **Cloud**, your data is processed by Langfuse under a [data processing addendum](/security/dpa). The work is contractual: security review, DPA signature, procurement. Operations, upgrades, scaling, and availability are Langfuse's problem.
- With **self-hosting**, data never leaves your environment, which can eliminate the vendor-review conversation entirely. The work is operational: you deploy, monitor, upgrade, and scale the stack yourself.

Neither is free. The right question is which kind of work your organization is better equipped to absorb.

## What each option is

### Langfuse Cloud

Managed service with four isolated regions as of September 2026: **EU** (Ireland, AWS `eu-west-1`, at `cloud.langfuse.com`), **US** (Oregon, `us-west-2`), **Japan** (Tokyo, `ap-northeast-1`), and a dedicated **HIPAA** region in Oregon with BAA support. Accounts and data are fully separated between regions; see [data regions and availability](/security/data-regions) for URLs, backup layers, and recovery objectives. Langfuse Cloud is SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 compliant and GDPR compliant; the [compliance FAQ](/security/compliance-faq) has the current list and how to obtain reports. Pricing is usage-based on [billable units](/docs/administration/billable-units), with a [calculator](/pricing?calculatorOpen=true) for estimation.

### Self-hosted (open source)

The MIT-licensed core includes all core Langfuse features and APIs without limits, on the same infrastructure design Langfuse Cloud runs: web and worker containers, Postgres, ClickHouse, Redis, and S3-compatible blob storage, with S3-first ingestion so events survive database unavailability. There is no scalability limitation relative to Cloud. Deployment guides cover [Docker Compose](/self-hosting/deployment/docker-compose) (for trials), [Kubernetes via Helm](/self-hosting/deployment/kubernetes-helm), and cloud-specific setups.

### Self-hosted Enterprise

Adds a [license key](/self-hosting/license-key) on top of the OSS deployment for enterprise features, plus commercial support. As of September 2026 the license-gated set is: project-level RBAC roles, protected prompt labels, data retention policies, audit logs, server-side data masking, UI customization, organization creators, the organization management API with SCIM, and the instance management API. The infrastructure and operations picture is identical to OSS self-hosting.

### A note on "BYOC"

Teams often ask for "bring your own cloud". In Langfuse terms that is self-hosting: the full stack runs in your cloud account, operated by you, optionally with an Enterprise license. If your requirement is "data stays in our account but someone else operates it", talk to the Langfuse team about what support arrangements are possible before assuming a fully managed in-VPC offering exists.

## Decision guide

Work through these in order; the first hard constraint decides.

1. **Regulated data with a mapped region.** Healthcare workloads with PHI: Cloud's HIPAA region with a BAA covers this without self-hosting. EU data-residency requirements: the EU region in Ireland covers most GDPR postures, and the [EU data residency guide](/resources/engineering/langfuse-eu-data-residency-gdpr) spells out exactly what stays where. Teams in Japan have the Tokyo region.
2. **Data may not leave your network at all.** Air-gapped environments, VPC-only policies, or regulator expectations that no third party processes the data: self-host. This is the one requirement Cloud cannot meet by design.
3. **No platform team, or the platform team is at capacity.** Running Postgres, ClickHouse, Redis, and blob storage in production is real work (sizing, upgrades, backups, disk management). If nobody owns that today, Cloud is the honest default; an under-operated observability stack fails exactly when you need it.
4. **Existing ClickHouse operations expertise.** Teams already running ClickHouse (or with a serious data-infra practice) lose the main self-hosting deterrent. Self-hosting becomes a straightforward cost-and-control calculation.
5. **Neither constraint applies.** Start with Cloud. Moving to self-hosted later is supported: the [data migration cookbook](/guides/cookbook/example_data_migration) has scripts that transfer traces, prompts, and datasets between projects, and most teams cut tracing over fresh anyway. This is not a one-way door.

## The operations reality of self-hosting

What you sign up for, concretely:

- **Five components to run**: Langfuse web and worker containers plus Postgres, ClickHouse, Redis, and S3-compatible storage. Managed versions of the databases (for example ClickHouse Cloud) remove much of the burden and are a common middle path.
- **ClickHouse is the part that needs respect.** Disk growth, partition and retention management, and upgrade sequencing are where self-hosters spend their operations time. Budget for someone to own it.
- **Upgrades are yours.** Langfuse ships frequently; plan a regular upgrade cadence and read the [upgrade guides](/self-hosting/upgrade) rather than jumping many versions at once.
- **Sizing depends on instrumentation.** Trace volume is driven by observations per trace, not requests. Measure a representative day before capacity planning (the [billable units](/docs/administration/billable-units) page shows how to count).

None of this is exotic for a team that runs stateful services. All of it is a poor fit for a two-person AI team without infrastructure support.

## Total cost of ownership, honestly

- **Cloud costs** scale with [usage](/pricing) and are fully visible up front. There is no infrastructure line item and no headcount cost.
- **Self-hosted costs** are infrastructure (compute, storage, the managed databases if you use them) plus the fraction of an engineer who owns the deployment. The software is free; the operation is not. For most teams the engineer-time component dominates.
- The crossover point depends almost entirely on whether platform capacity already exists. Adding observability to an existing, well-run Kubernetes and data estate costs little at the margin. Standing up that estate to avoid a SaaS bill rarely pays.

## FAQ

### Is self-hosted Langfuse feature-limited compared to Cloud? [#feature-limited]

No for core features: tracing, evaluation, prompt management, datasets, and dashboards are all in the MIT-licensed OSS version without limits. A set of enterprise features requires an Enterprise license key on self-hosted deployments; as of September 2026 that set is project-level RBAC roles, protected prompt labels, data retention policies, audit logs, server-side data masking, UI customization, organization creators, the organization management API with SCIM, and the instance management API. The [license key page](/self-hosting/license-key) is the authoritative list.

### Can I move from Cloud to self-hosted later, or the reverse? [#move-between]

Yes. The [data migration cookbook](/guides/cookbook/example_data_migration) provides Python scripts that transfer traces, prompts, and datasets between projects, including between a Cloud project and a self-hosted instance. Most teams cut tracing over fresh rather than importing history. Plan the move around a low-stakes date and run both briefly in parallel if continuity matters.

### Does self-hosting scale to high trace volumes? [#scale]

Yes. Self-hosted Langfuse uses the same architecture as Langfuse Cloud, and there are no scalability limitations between the versions. Scale is an infrastructure-sizing question (primarily ClickHouse and blob storage), not a software-edition question.

### Which option is faster to start with? [#faster-start]

Cloud: sign up, create a project, send a trace. A self-hosted trial via [Docker Compose](/self-hosting/deployment/docker-compose) takes under an hour, but treat it as an evaluation environment; production self-hosting deserves the full [deployment guide](/self-hosting).

### Do I need Kubernetes to self-host? [#kubernetes]

No, but production deployments typically use the [Helm chart on Kubernetes](/self-hosting/deployment/kubernetes-helm). Docker Compose is documented for trials and small setups rather than production.

### Which Cloud region should I pick? [#which-region]

Pick the region that matches your data-residency requirement first: EU for GDPR-driven residency, the HIPAA region for PHI under a BAA, Japan for teams that need data in Tokyo, US otherwise. Regions are fully separated, including user accounts, so switching later means creating a new account and migrating data with the cookbook scripts. Prompt fetch latency is a secondary factor because the SDKs cache prompts client-side.

You can deploy Langfuse v4 on [Pethost](https://pethost.dev/), a small cloud for personal projects, with its one-click template.
The template runs the services of the [Docker Compose deployment](/self-hosting/deployment/docker-compose) on one machine, so the same limits apply: no high availability and no scaling.
See the [architecture overview](/self-hosting#architecture) for more details.

## Deploy

Open the [Langfuse template](https://pethost.dev/templates/langfuse/), click **Deploy on Pethost**, and enter the email and password of the first user.

The template creates these services on the machine:

- Langfuse v4 web and worker services
- PostgreSQL 17
- Redis 7
- ClickHouse, with its system logs kept for 7 days
- MinIO, reachable only from the other services

It generates `NEXTAUTH_SECRET`, `SALT`, `ENCRYPTION_KEY` and the data stores' passwords, and sets `NEXTAUTH_URL` to the project's HTTPS address.
It uses [headless initialization](/self-hosting/administration/headless-initialization) to create an organization and a project named Default, with the email and password you entered as their owner, and sets `AUTH_DISABLE_SIGNUP=true`.

In a smoke test the six services used about 2.7 GB of memory at rest, with the Node.js heap of the web service bounded to 1 GB and the worker's to 512 MB; ingestion and queries need more.
The [Docker Compose guide](/self-hosting/deployment/docker-compose) recommends at least 4 cores and 16 GiB of memory, which Pethost's Scale plan (8 vCPU, 30 GB of memory) covers.
On such a machine, raise the heap bounds (`NODE_OPTIONS`) in the project's `compose.yaml`.

MinIO has no public endpoint, so media uploads from browsers and SDK clients do not work until you configure [blob storage](/self-hosting/deployment/infrastructure/blobstorage) that they can reach.

The template's files are in the [template repository](https://github.com/pethost-dev/templates/tree/main/langfuse).
